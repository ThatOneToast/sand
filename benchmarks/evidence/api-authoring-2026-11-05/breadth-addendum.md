# Breadth-gap addendum — Vanilla Expedition

The design brief starts from what a vanilla datapack might express, not from the Sand APIs encountered first. The completed slice samples many categories, but leaves substantial vanilla capability unexplored. This is a scope inventory, not an authoritative census of every Minecraft 26.3 registry or field: no internet or native registry inspection was permitted.

The original assessment and datapack remain unchanged. This addendum uses only the original frozen `sand api export` catalog. `breadth-catalog-probe.txt` and `breadth-contracts.txt` preserve the additional read-only probes. No new build or native run was performed.

## Status definitions

- **Unattempted:** no authoring/export exercise for this capability. Finding a catalog declaration now does not promote it to implementation or verification.
- **Probed/deferred:** the original exercise inspected relevant contracts or attempted an adjacent workflow, but deliberately stopped short of implementing this capability.
- **Discovery gap:** the frozen catalog probe did not reveal the needed authoring contract. This does not prove that Sand, an extension hook, or vanilla cannot express it.
- **Demonstrated unsupported:** a concrete authoring/native operation was attempted and evidence established lack of support. **No entire resource family below earned this classification.**

## Meaning of the breadth score

The original **8/10 coverage score is a qualitative usability judgment about the breadth of typed authoring surfaces encountered and the ease of exporting representative categories**. It is **not “80% of vanilla coverage.”** There was no defined denominator of vanilla registries, commands, schema fields, legal combinations or observable behaviors; families differ enormously in size; no random or exhaustive sample was taken; and no native correctness pass occurred. A simple loot table or a flat dimension counts as a category example, not completion of that category.

A defensible percentage would require a version-specific inventory, explicit weighting, operation/field-level tests, and native acceptance/behavior results. This exercise supplies none of those denominators. The 8/10 should therefore be read alongside the gap inventory, not substituted for it. The strongest supported statement is: **the CLI enabled a broad, connected, typed vertical slice, while much of vanilla's data-driven and runtime surface remains unattempted or unverified.**
